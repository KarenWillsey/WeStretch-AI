# CMO — Campaign Planning: Multiplayer Stretching

_Skill applied: `CMO/skills/campaign-planning`_

## Objective and audience

Objective: drive activation of the new social feature among **existing users** first (invite-a-friend loop), then use it as an acquisition hook for **new users** once there's a base of active "stretch parties" to join. Primary audience for launch: existing engaged users (streak ≥ 7 days) who are the most likely to have real relationships to invite into a stretch party.

## Channel plan

- **In-app first**: launch is primarily an in-app moment (contextual prompt after a completed routine: "stretch with a friend tomorrow?") rather than paid acquisition — this is a retention/engagement feature, not a standalone acquisition product, so budget should be light at launch. *(Illustrative estimate, not a confirmed budget — see [cfo.md](cfo.md).)*
- **Owned channels (email/push)**: announce to the existing user base, segmented to lead with engaged users first to seed initial stretch parties before the feature looks empty to newer users.
- **Paid/social**: hold until (1) the feature has a real usage story to show (e.g., "10,000 stretch parties completed") and (2) the CGO's experiment (see [cgo.md](cgo.md)) confirms retention lift — don't spend acquisition budget on an unvalidated mechanic.

## Creative angle

Core hook: *"Stretching is easier when someone's counting on you."* Lean into consistency and community rather than performance/competition — this matches WeStretch's existing brand tone (per [cxo.md](cxo.md)'s note on avoiding accountability-shaming) and differentiates from competitor apps that frame social fitness as leaderboard competition.

## Success metrics

- **Primary**: % of active users who send or accept a stretch-party invite within 30 days of launch.
- **Secondary**: invite acceptance rate (signal of whether the ask itself is compelling) and whether invited non-users convert to signups (acquisition-loop viability).
- **Kill/scale decision point**: if invite acceptance rate is very low, the issue is likely the invite UX/moment, not the channel — coordinate with [cxo.md](cxo.md) before increasing spend.
