# Robot Mower and Mound Research

## Recommended landscape architecture

Use an **informal naturalistic perimeter frame**.

- Repeat the current yard's loose curves, widening around tree anchors and
  narrowing between them.
- Vary mound width, length and height. Do not mirror the parcels exactly.
- Use two low tree anchors per parcel and repeat the same limited plant palette.
- Keep the landform continuous so it reads as one landscape from the road.
- Keep the centre as one simple, uninterrupted grass room.

This approach is supported by established landscape principles:

- Curved lines create an informal, natural character. Low horizontal lines can
  visually tie spaces together. [University of Florida IFAS](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/MG086)
- Repeating line, form and texture creates rhythm and unity between separate
  areas. [Virginia Cooperative Extension](https://pressbooks.lib.vt.edu/emgtraining/chapter/16/)
- Plant masses can frame a central open lawn. Simplicity means creating useful
  spaces instead of filling every area. [NC State Extension](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/extension-gardener-handbook/19-landscape-design)
- Natural-looking berms should be asymmetric, use large curves and commonly
  stay about four to six times longer than they are wide. A height around 18 to
  24 inches is a useful residential starting point. [University of California Agriculture and Natural Resources](https://ucanr.edu/blog/real-dirt/article/landscaping-berms)

## Affordable mound strategy

- Shape the design mainly with clean local fill and a topsoil cap.
- Use long, shallow mounds instead of tall planted beds.
- Let the mounds do most of the visual work. Plants are accents, not the frame.
- Use two trees, three small shrub groups and two perennial drifts per parcel.
- Keep all new grades inside the property line and blend back to the existing
  grade before the boundary.
- Confirm drainage before moving soil. New grading must not push water toward
  the house, road or neighbouring land.

The final height and slope must be checked against the selected mower. Mower
slope capacity varies by model. Trees on steep mower slopes cause extra turns,
slipping and turf wear. [Husqvarna slope guidance](https://us-support.husqvarna.com/en/automower/KA-01431)

## Best planting method for a robot mower

### Preferred approach under the current material rules

1. Treat each curved mound as one continuous no-mow planting zone.
2. Keep the robot on the flatter central lawn and lower mound shoulder.
3. Place the mower boundary just outside the dense planted edge, following the
   exact distance required by the chosen mower.
4. Plant low groundcover densely to the lawn edge so there is no exposed soil,
   wood chip or gravel strip.
5. Keep shrub branches and perennial foliage behind the mower boundary at
   mature size.
6. Keep the tree root flare visible. Do not pile soil or plants against trunks.
7. Use broad convex curves. Avoid tight inward pockets where the mower cannot
   turn or reach.
8. Make both parcels part of one mapped work area when the mower and access
   route allow it. Avoid separate little islands.

Husqvarna recommends boundary islands around beds and exposed tree roots. It
also notes that trees without exposed roots may not require an island.
[Husqvarna island guidance](https://us-support.husqvarna.com/en/automower/KA-01427)

For boundary-wire models, narrow passages need model-specific clearance.
Husqvarna commonly recommends 2 metres between boundary wires without a guide
wire, or 60 centimetres with one. Some models need more.
[Husqvarna passage guidance](https://us-support.husqvarna.com/en/automower/KA-01710)

### Important limitation

With only grass and plants, **zero edge trimming cannot be guaranteed**.

- A standard robot mower leaves some grass beside a no-mow boundary because
  its blade sits inside the wheel track.
- A mower with a true edge-cutting disc reduces this strip, but still depends
  on accurate boundary placement. [Husqvarna EdgeCut guidance](https://us-support.husqvarna.com/en/automower/KA-70235)
- Dense low groundcover can hide and suppress the remaining edge. Expect hand
  pulling or clipping during establishment and occasional cleanup later.
- The only reliable zero-trimming construction is a flush hard mowing strip
  that the mower can overlap. That conflicts with the current grass-and-plants
  material rule, so it is not included in the design.

## Tree and shrub bases

Use the sample photos as **shape and coverage references only**. The exact
groundcover species must be chosen for local climate, sun, water and the mature
tree canopy.

The desired base is:

- Dense living groundcover or closely spaced low perennials.
- No turf against the trunk.
- No exposed wood chips or gravel.
- A visible tree root flare and an unplanted breathing space around the trunk.
- One flowing planted mass rather than a separate ring around every plant.

Turf often performs poorly beneath trees because of shade and competition for
water and nutrients. Penn State recommends nongrass groundcovers where turf is
not practical. [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/growing-turf-under-shaded-conditions)

Do not raise soil around an established tree or bury its root flare. Start
underplanting away from the trunk and avoid damaging large roots.
[Penn State underplanting guidance](https://extension.psu.edu/underplanting-trees-respect-the-roots)
