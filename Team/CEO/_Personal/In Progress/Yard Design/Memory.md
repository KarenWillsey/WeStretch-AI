# Yard Design Memory

## Confirmed preferences

- The east and west parcels will eventually contain houses, but may remain open
  for several years.
- The temporary landscape should read as part of the current home property.
- The current yard's raised, irregular tree islands are the strongest feature
  to repeat.
- The new parcels should remain mostly grass for play and easy care.
- A robot lawnmower will maintain both parcels.
- Planting should use a minimal number of trees, shrubs and perennials.
- No wood chips or gravel are permitted.
- Planting must stay completely inside the green parcel boundaries.
- Perimeter framing and continuity with the existing yard are essential.
- The middle of each parcel must remain open for playing.
- Mounds must use broad, uneven curves with no straight bed lines or sharp
  angles. They should widen around trees and narrow between planting groups.
- Earth shaping should provide most of the design impact because this is a
  temporary, cost-conscious landscape.
- The parcels should feel connected from the road through repeated mound
  rhythm, with far fewer plants than the established yard.
- Step 1 must establish the mound forms before any plants are placed.
- Corner mounds should be separate, strongly curved landforms with open grass
  between them, not one continuous perimeter mound.
