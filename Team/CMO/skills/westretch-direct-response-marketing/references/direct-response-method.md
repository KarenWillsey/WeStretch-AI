# Direct-response method

This method adapts the 11-part process described by Sabri Suby in [Give Me 27 Minutes and I'll Make You Disgustingly Good at Digital Marketing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HsjuAvqs3zU) and the sequence in his [17-Step Secret Selling System](https://kingkong.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/17-Step-Selling-System.pdf).

Use the method as a marketing operating system, not as a claim that every tactic or benchmark will fit every budget, platform, or business.

## The 11-part operating system

1. **Master the foundation:** Marketing must capture attention and convert that attention.
2. **Study classic direct response:** Review five to ten old print ads daily for 30 days. Extract the headline, hook, promise, proof, offer, risk reversal, and CTA.
3. **Separate demand capture from demand generation:** Search reaches existing intent; social advertising can create demand at larger scale.
4. **Study modern media:** Make ads resemble content the audience already consumes. Use AIDA.
5. **Build an industry swipe file:** Save long-running ads, full landing pages, funnels, angles, offers, and calls to action.
6. **Conduct Halo research:** Gather emotionally charged language about pains, fears, hopes, desires, objections, and failed alternatives.
7. **Launch and learn:** Build skill through frequent static-ad repetitions. A useful production target is five concepts per working day or 25 per week.
8. **Match market awareness:** Adjust the argument for ready-to-buy, product-aware, solution-aware, problem-aware, and unaware audiences.
9. **Use the 17-step selling sequence:** Build the page in the order below.
10. **Continuously test the funnel:** Form a hypothesis, compare it with the control, make a decision, and repeat.
11. **Remain a student of markets:** Study advertising inside and outside the category.

## Swipe-file system

Maintain two libraries.

### Classic direct-response file

For every useful example record:

- Audience
- Headline formula
- Hook
- Promise
- Emotional driver
- Proof
- Offer
- Risk reversal
- CTA
- Adaptable principle

### Modern industry file

Capture:

- Advertiser and category
- First-seen and last-seen dates
- Ad-library link when available
- Creative screenshot
- Awareness level
- Hook and angle
- Promise and mechanism
- Proof
- Offer and pricing frame
- CTA
- Landing-page screenshots
- Why it may be surviving

Longevity suggests an ad may work, but does not prove profitability. Treat it as a research clue.

## Halo research

### Research question

For a typical core campaign:

> What does a woman 50-65 say, feel, fear, and want when stiffness or recurring discomfort begins limiting normal life, but she is not yet searching for a stretching app?

Adapt the question when the user specifies another audience or problem.

### Sources

- WeStretch support, surveys, cancellations, reviews, and usage notes
- Competitor app reviews
- Reddit and Quora
- YouTube comments
- Reviews of stretching, mobility, physiotherapy, yoga, Pilates, and related products
- Search suggestions and related queries
- Customer interviews and approved community discussions

### Categories

- Pains
- Fears
- Near-term hopes
- Identity-level dreams
- Objections
- Failed alternatives
- Trigger moments
- Exact phrases with headline potential

### Evidence target for a full research sprint

- 200 useful excerpts
- 50 pains
- 30 fears
- 40 desired outcomes
- 25 objections
- 25 complaints about failed solutions
- 20 trigger moments
- 10 strong verbatim phrases

Rank insights by frequency, emotional intensity, product relevance, and evidence strength.

## Initial 25-ad matrix for WeStretch

Create five executions for each angle:

1. **Everyday warning signs:** Socks, stairs, car exit, chair rise, floor rise
2. **Back discomfort and stiffness:** Long drive, desk, morning, sleep, travel
3. **Keep doing what you love:** Grandchildren, gardening, golf, pickleball, travel
4. **Personalized today:** Time, body area, position, goal, current ability
5. **Why generic videos fail:** Fixed length, wrong movements, repetition, no adaptation, no progression

Vary the awareness level, hook, visual, and proof. Do not create 25 superficial rewrites of the same ad.

## The 17-step sales-page sequence

Run the sequence in order unless there is a tested reason to depart from it.

1. **Call out the audience:** State who the message is for.
2. **Demand attention:** Lead with one large, specific, believable benefit.
3. **Support the promise:** Use a clear subheadline to explain why it is plausible.
4. **Create intrigue:** Write 10-20 bullets and keep the strongest six.
5. **Make the problem vivid:** Use customer language and failed attempts without shaming.
6. **Reveal the solution:** Explain how WeStretch resolves the shortcomings of alternatives.
7. **Establish credentials:** Use verifiable expertise, results, ratings, press, or scale.
8. **Translate features into benefits:** Build a feature-to-benefit table, then lead with benefits.
9. **Add social proof:** Match proof to objections and preserve authentic meaning.
10. **Present the core offer:** State exactly what the customer receives and why it matters.
11. **Add two or three genuine bonuses:** Include only deliverables the team can provide immediately.
12. **Stack the value:** Explain the combined usefulness without invented dollar values.
13. **Reveal the price:** State current price, billing, renewal, and trial terms clearly.
14. **Add genuine urgency:** Use only a real deadline, quantity, price change, or time-bound bonus.
15. **Remove risk:** Explain the trial and cancellation. Add a guarantee only when operations support it.
16. **Issue one CTA:** Use one direct command and repeat it consistently.
17. **Close with a P.S.:** Restate outcome, offer, risk reversal, consequence of delay, and CTA concisely.

The highest-leverage page elements are normally the headline, subheadline, offer, section subheadlines, and close.

## Testing cadence

### Initial 30 days

| Time | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Days 1-2 | Verify facts, tracking, control, and baseline |
| Days 3-7 | Complete and rank Halo research |
| Day 8 | Approve one audience, promise, mechanism, and offer |
| Days 9-10 | Write awareness-based messages and five angles |
| Days 11-15 | Produce 25 static ads and the 17-step landing page |
| Days 16-22 | Run the first controlled acquisition test |
| Days 23-30 | Interpret results and launch the next test batch |

### Weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** Review the funnel and write hypotheses.
- **Tuesday-Thursday:** Launch tests, create new ads, and update research.
- **Friday:** Make keep, iterate, stop, or scale decisions and update the control.

### Daily production target

For a high-volume sprint, create five static concepts each working day. At low spend, use CTR and page engagement for early message screening, not as proof of subscription profitability.

## Diagnostic logic

| Pattern | Likely issue | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Low CTR | Hook, image, or relevance | Test a stronger trigger moment or desired outcome |
| High CTR, weak page engagement | Message mismatch, page confusion, or poor speed | Repeat the promise above the fold and simplify |
| Good engagement, few trials | Weak offer, unclear terms, or inadequate proof | Improve offer clarity, proof, and risk reversal |
| Trials, few first routines | Onboarding friction or expectation mismatch | Improve the handoff and first-routine guidance |
| First routines, weak paid conversion | Habit, value, pricing, or nurture issue | Analyze behavior and lifecycle communication |
| Paid conversion, rapid cancellations | Wrong audience or overpromising | Tighten claims and examine the early product experience |

## 90-day definition of done

- Working classic and modern swipe files
- Ranked voice-of-customer database
- One documented primary audience and acquisition message
- At least 250 static-ad concepts across ten production weeks
- A regular Pro page using the 17-step sequence
- An experiment log with named controls
- Five meaningful tests active or queued
- Documented winners and failures
- Reliable cost-per-trial, paid-CAC, cancellation, and retention numbers
- A repeatable keep, iterate, stop, and scale process
