## Chase's strategy note

**Angle already in the draft (good instinct):** this challenge is built on **self-generated proof** — "give it 15 minutes a day for 28 days and see for yourself" — which is Chase's most honest and durable persuasion technique. This didn't need inventing; it's already the spine of the page.

- **FATE lever:** Focus (one clear ask, 28 days) and Emotion (relief from things you've been quietly working around), with Identity underneath — "I'm someone who gives my body what it needs before it becomes a crisis," not "I'm someone who fixed a problem."
- **Six-Axis read:** the page raises openness (relatable body moments) and expectancy (the "what if" reveal) *before* asking for compliance (payment) — correct sequencing. The one gap: it never raises **trust/authority** before that ask. Nothing on the page says *why* the personalization can be believed until after you've already paid.
- **Ethical check:** passes. No manufactured urgency, no fear-stacking, no fake authority. The only issue is a missed opportunity, not a violation.

## Loop 2 — after Expert's rewrite

Only the pieces below changed. Everything else stayed as written in the original (already strong).

**Reveal section** — cut "simply," paired category language with filmable specifics:

> What if there were less to work around?
>
> Getting up without your back announcing itself. Reaching the top shelf without thinking about your shoulder first. Less adjusting around whatever happens to be bothering you today.
>
> Give your body 15 minutes a day for 28 days and see how much better it can feel.

**How It Works** — added the mechanism, honestly attributed:

> **1. Tell WeStretch what feels tight.**
> Physiotherapists already mapped which stretches are safe and in what order. WeStretch uses what you tell it, plus your goals and your time, to hand you today's routine from that plan.
>
> **2. Follow along for 15 minutes a day.**
> An animated guide walks you through every movement in real time, so you're never guessing at a still picture. Just follow along.
>
> **3. Stay consistent.**
> Keep showing up for weekly prize chances and two grand prizes.

**New FAQ entry** (inserted after "What if I'm really stiff…"):

> **Is this actually safe for me?**
> Every stretch was developed and validated with physiotherapists and movement specialists, who mapped which stretches are safe and in what order. WeStretch uses what you tell it about what's bothering you to decide which of those stretches you get. If something's off-limits for you, it's left out.

**Subscription screen subhead** — one clause added right before the price choice, where trust matters most:

> Give your body 28 days. See how much better it can feel.
>
> You've probably stretched when something starts bothering you. The difference this time is you're not stopping after a day or two.
>
> For 28 days, you'll follow a routine physiotherapists helped map, built around what's bothering you, for 15 minutes a day. Enough time to stop reacting to whatever feels tight today and find out what consistency can actually change.

**"Everything you get" checklist** — one line updated for consistency:

> ✓ Personalized, physio-informed stretching focused on what's bothering you
> ✓ Progress tracked throughout the challenge
> ✓ Chance to win weekly prizes and one of two grand prizes
> ✓ Full access to the WeStretch app starting today

- **Believe, page-wide:** can't reach A+ everywhere. `positioning.md` confirms no quantitative outcome proof exists yet (no user counts, retention, or results data). The page correctly never fabricates this — that's the right call, not a defect.

Same logic `voice.md` applies to "joint": precise where earned, a speed bump where skimmed.
