## Product Overview

WeStretch is a stretching and mobility app for adults who have noticed their body
getting in the way of a life that is still very much underway.

Every pose in the library was developed and validated with physiotherapists and
movement specialists. The app assembles each routine from that expert-decided
progression using the user's goals, the time they have, and what they have
already completed. Hold times and difficulty step up on a schedule.

An animated guide takes the user through every movement, so however complex the
logic underneath, their only job is to follow the next instruction.

**Tiers:** Guest, Free, Pro.

## Problems we solve

Written as the user would describe them, not as clinical outcomes.

- Getting out of the car and thinking about your hip
- Getting up off the floor and it becoming an event
- Checking a blind spot by turning your whole body
- Putting a bag in the overhead bin and feeling the shoulder object
- A long flight leaving you unable to enjoy the first day of the trip
- The massage wearing off by Tuesday

**The mechanism worth explaining:** too much sitting, too little movement, so
muscles gradually tighten.

## Differentiation

**The claim no competitor can honestly make: a physiotherapist decided this
order.**

Competitors say "personalised". Personalised almost always means an algorithm
guessing from behaviour. Ours means clinicians mapped the progression in advance
and the app delivers it on schedule.

That is both more honest and a stronger trust claim. Lead with it.

See `voice.md`, "Evolves with you", for the exact wording this must and must not
be expressed in. Getting this claim wrong is the single easiest way to say
something factually untrue about the product.

## Objections

| Objection | Our answer |
|---|---|
| "I've tried stretching apps and stopped." | Consistency beats intensity, and the ask can be a single stretch. |
| "I don't have time." | Ten minutes, and one movement counts. |
| "Is this actually safe for me?" | Physio backed, explained rather than asserted. |
| "Am I too old for this?" | Never engage this framing. Age is not the problem and we never write as though it is. |
