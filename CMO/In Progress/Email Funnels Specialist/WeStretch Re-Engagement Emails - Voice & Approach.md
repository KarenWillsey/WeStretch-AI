## **WeStretch Re-Engagement Emails** *Voice & approach* 

**Who they reach:** an active subscriber who was stretching consistently, then stopped. They already believe stretching is good for them. Nothing here needs to convince them it matters.

**The only job:** make starting again today feel easy. Every line should lower the resistance to opening the app, never raise the pressure to. If a sentence adds pressure, cut it.

### **The voice**

Tight, warm, concrete, and relentlessly positive. Every email is pinned to a five-minute ask, and to the life the movement protects, not the app.

### **What defines it**

* **Five minutes.** State it plainly ("Five minutes, whenever you're ready," "Five minutes is plenty," "a small down payment"). It is the anchor, because it makes restarting feel easy.

* **No downside.** Cut anything that hints at decline, stakes, or guilt. Talk only about what there is to gain, never what is lost by stopping.

* **Concrete and sensory.** Trade concepts for images from real life: getting up off the floor without a second thought, a long walk, reaching to check your blind spot.

* **Warm and human.** Light contractions, a friendly opener, the occasional small metaphor ("a small vote for the direction you want").

* **The life, not the app.** The payoff is always the life underneath. Not the streak, not the app, the life the movement protects.

* **Consistent details.** Sign off "The WeStretch Team" with no dash. The CTA is "Start today's routine"

### **How to write one**

* **One idea, start to finish.** Never three lessons in one email.

* **Open with something obviously true.** "It's been about a week." "Life gets busy." The reader agrees before anything is asked, so it never feels like a pitch.

* **Observation, explanation, invitation.** A true opener, a plain reason, a gentle nudge. Just enough to make today's action feel obvious, never a lecture.

* **Never guilt.** No "you quit," no "you should have kept going." Normalize the lapse: you haven't fallen behind.

* **Ask for less, not more.** No mat needed. Nothing to catch up on. One stretch counts. The product asks for less effort, not more.

* **Movement, not exercise.** Move, mobility, stiffness, everyday movement, feeling good. Never workout, fitness, or performance. The promise is living well, not training.

* **Let the app remove decisions.** When you mention it, describe what it takes off the reader's plate: tell it how you feel and it builds around that. Functional, never hyped.

* **End on something achievable.** "Start today's routine." "Start where you are." Never a call to transform anything.

### **Before it goes out**

* Does it open with something the reader already knows is true?  
* Is there exactly one idea?  
* Does it make starting easier, or does it quietly ask for more?  
* Would the calm friend say it, or does it sound like a coach?  
* Any guilt, downside, or fitness language hiding in it? Cut it.

### **The three flows**

Five emails, sent at days 7, 11, 16, 23, and 30 after the last completed stretch. Any completed stretch ends the sequence. There are three interchangeable flows, each with a different theme in every slot, so a subscriber who lapses more than once gets a fresh set rather than the same emails again.

| Day | Flow 1 | Flow 2 | Flow 3 |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 7 | Feeling good is built | You've done this before | Tune back in |
| 11 | Nothing to catch up on | Small and often | One stretch is enough |
| 16 | Don't wait for motivation | Fits wherever you are | A reset for your head too |
| 23 | Your body adapts | It's the after that counts | Built only for you |
| 30 | The life it protects | Your future self | A body you don't think about |
